About Surprise

What It's Actually Like Living in Surprise, AZ

Surprise was founded in 1938 by Flora Mae Statler on farmland along what's now Grand Avenue, and incorporated as a city in 1960. For decades it stayed a small farming community anchored by retirement living, then grew rapidly from the late 1990s onward into one of the larger cities in the West Valley, with a population now well over 150,000.

The city is best known for Surprise Stadium, the spring training home of the Kansas City Royals and Texas Rangers, and for Sun City Grand (now branded The Grand), a large 55+ master-planned community that helped define the area before the wave of family-oriented development along Loop 303. Today, Surprise is a mix of both, active adult communities alongside newer family neighborhoods like Marley Park, Asante, and Surprise Farms.

Moving to Surprise typically means trading a longer commute to central Phoenix for more house, newer construction, and a slower pace. Loop 303 and Grand Avenue (US-60) are the main routes in and out, and the city has grown enough retail and job base of its own, anchored by the Village at Prasada, that it functions less like a bedroom community than it used to.

Neighborhoods & Communities in Surprise

A few of the master-planned communities and areas people ask about most when they're looking at Surprise real estate.

Marley Park

Master-PlannedWalkableEstablished

A 950-acre master-planned community in central Surprise built out between 2005 and 2020, known for tree-lined streets, front-porch architecture, themed parks, and the Heritage Club amenity center.

Asante & Sterling Grove

New ConstructionMultiple BuildersNorth Surprise

Newer master plans in north Surprise, with Sterling Grove offering a guard-gated, golf-anchored setting and Asante bringing more new-construction inventory across several builders.

Sun City Grand (The Grand)

55+GolfEstablished

An age-qualified 55+ community with multiple golf courses, clubhouses, and an active social calendar. One of the communities that put Surprise on the map before the newer family developments arrived.

An Honest Look

Living in Surprise: What to Weigh

Worth Knowing

  • Newer housing stock overall, with active new construction in several master-planned communities
  • Loop 303 gives fairly direct access across the whole West Valley
  • A wide range of price points, from entry-level to upscale gated communities
  • Strong options for both families and active adult (55+) buyers
  • Growing retail and job base along the Prasada corridor, less of a bedroom-community feel than it used to be

Things to Consider

  • Longer commute to central Phoenix and Sky Harbor than East Valley or Central Phoenix locations
  • HOAs are common in most communities, with dues and design guidelines that vary widely
  • Heavier seasonal traffic from October through April with the snowbird population
  • Newer areas can mean fewer mature trees and less established landscaping
  • Summer heat is a factor here as it is across the whole Valley

New Construction

Building in Surprise

New construction in Surprise is concentrated mostly in the northern part of the city, in communities like Asante and along the Loop 303 corridor, where several builders including Lennar, Fulton Homes, and Taylor Morrison have active projects. Home styles here tend to run newer and more energy-efficient than Surprise's older, established sections closer to Bell Road and Grand Avenue.

If you're considering a new-build home in Surprise, you're welcome to have your own real estate representation, even when buying directly from a builder. It's worth thinking about who represents your interests before you visit a model home or register with a builder's sales office.
Schools & Education

School Districts Serving Surprise

Surprise is served by several public school districts, and which one applies depends on the specific address, since district boundaries don't always follow city lines exactly.

  • Dysart Unified School District (covers most of Surprise)
  • Peoria Unified School District (parts of southeast Surprise)
  • A number of charter school options throughout the area

For families relocating to Surprise, Estrella Mountain Community College and West-MEC's career and technical programs are both within a reasonable drive for older students.

We're not able to advise on which schools or districts are the "best" fit, since that depends entirely on your family's needs. We're happy to point you toward district boundary maps and third-party research tools like GreatSchools.org so you can look into specific schools yourself.
Location

Getting Around Surprise

Surprise sits in the northwest Valley, bordered by El Mirage and Youngtown to the east, Peoria to the southeast, and unincorporated Maricopa County to the north and west.

  • Loop 303 is the main north-south route through the city
  • Grand Avenue (US-60) cuts diagonally through Surprise toward Wickenburg
  • Bell Road carries east-west traffic to Sun City, Peoria, and Glendale
  • About 35-40 minutes to Downtown Phoenix
  • About 40-45 minutes to Phoenix Sky Harbor Airport
  • Heavier seasonal traffic from snowbirds between October and April

FAQ

Common Questions About Surprise

What is it like living in Surprise, Arizona?

Surprise is a fast-growing West Valley city made up mostly of master-planned communities, with a mix of family neighborhoods and large 55+ active adult sections.

What types of homes are available in Surprise?

Mostly single-family homes within planned communities, ranging from entry-level new construction to gated and age-restricted sections.

Does Surprise have new construction?

Yes, primarily in the northern part of the city, in communities like Asante and Sterling Grove along the Loop 303 corridor.

How far is Surprise from Downtown Phoenix?

About 35 to 40 minutes by car via Loop 303 or Grand Avenue, depending on traffic and the specific part of Surprise.

How far is Surprise from Phoenix Sky Harbor Airport?

About 40 to 45 minutes under normal traffic conditions.

What are some popular neighborhoods in Surprise?

Marley Park, Asante, Sterling Grove, and Sun City Grand are among the more well-known master-planned communities.

What is there to do in Surprise?

Surprise Stadium, the Village at Prasada, and the White Tank Mountains are among the most visited spots.

What should I know before moving to Surprise?

Most neighborhoods have an HOA, so it's worth understanding the dues and rules for a specific community before you buy. It's also worth knowing that the commute to central Phoenix is longer than from East Valley cities, and that some communities are age-restricted.
