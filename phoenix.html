About Phoenix

What It's Actually Like Living in Phoenix, AZ

Phoenix is the fifth-largest city in the country by population, and it doesn't behave like one city so much as a patchwork of very different ones. Central neighborhoods like Willo, Encanto-Palmcroft, and the Downtown core have historic homes on tree-lined streets and easy access to light rail. Head further out and the city opens into newer, more spread-out development in North Phoenix and Ahwatukee, closer in feel to the suburbs than to downtown.

The Roosevelt Row Arts District and the area around Chase Field and Footprint Center have seen real investment over the past decade, with more restaurants, breweries, and residential high-rises than Phoenix has traditionally had downtown. That said, most of the city is still low-rise and car-dependent, which is part of what keeps home prices more approachable here than in Scottsdale or Paradise Valley.

Moving to Phoenix Arizona means choosing which version of the city fits you, historic and walkable, newer and suburban, or somewhere in between, since the range within city limits is bigger than almost anywhere else in the Valley.

North Phoenix

Newer, More Spread Out

Master-planned communities, newer construction, and larger lots than you'll find closer to downtown. Areas like Desert Ridge and Deer Valley are popular with buyers who want a suburban feel within Phoenix city limits.

An Honest Look

Living in Phoenix: What to Weigh

Worth Knowing

  • Enormous range of home styles, ages, and price points within one city
  • Central location with quick access to nearly everywhere else in the Valley
  • Growing downtown with light rail, sports venues, and a real arts scene
  • Historic, tree-lined neighborhoods close to the urban core
  • Generally more affordable than Scottsdale or Paradise Valley for comparable square footage

Things to Consider

  • Quality and age of housing stock varies enormously block to block
  • Some central neighborhoods have older infrastructure and smaller lots
  • Summer heat is more intense in the urban core due to the heat-island effect
  • Traffic on surface streets and freeways can be heavy at peak times
  • Being such a large city, it takes real legwork to figure out which pocket actually fits you
Real Estate in Phoenix

What's Available

Phoenix has the widest range of housing in the Valley, which is both its biggest advantage and the reason it takes more research to narrow down.

Historic Homes

Bungalows and ranch homes in districts like Willo, Encanto, and Coronado, many with historic preservation guidelines.

Single-Family Homes

The majority of the city's housing, ranging from modest to large executive homes depending on the area.

Condos & High-Rises

Concentrated downtown and along the light rail corridor, popular with buyers who want a low-maintenance, urban lifestyle.

New Construction

Ongoing in North Phoenix and parts of Ahwatukee, though less concentrated than in the outer suburbs.

Luxury Homes

Found in pockets like Arcadia's edges, the Biltmore area, and parts of North Central Phoenix.

Townhomes

A growing share of the market, especially in infill developments near downtown and midtown.

New Construction

Building in Phoenix

New construction within Phoenix city limits is more limited than in the outer suburbs, since most available land has already been developed. What exists tends to be infill projects, downtown condo and apartment towers, and pockets of new single-family development in North Phoenix.

If you're considering a new-build home in Phoenix, you're welcome to have your own real estate representation, even when buying directly from a builder. It's worth thinking about who represents your interests before you visit a model home or register with a builder's sales office.
Schools & Education

School Districts Serving Phoenix

Phoenix is served by a large number of public school districts and charter schools, since the city is so spread out. Which district applies depends entirely on the specific address.

  • Phoenix Union High School District
  • Osborn, Madison, and Washington Elementary School Districts (among others)
  • Numerous charter school options throughout the city
  • Several private school options in central and North Phoenix

Phoenix is home to Arizona State University's Downtown campus, Grand Canyon University, and several community college campuses.

We're not able to advise on which schools or districts are the "best" fit, since that depends entirely on your family's needs. We're happy to point you toward district boundary maps and third-party research tools like GreatSchools.org so you can look into specific schools yourself.
Location

Getting Around Phoenix

Phoenix sits at the center of the Valley, bordered by Scottsdale, Tempe, Glendale, Paradise Valley, and Ahwatukee, with most other Valley cities within a 20 to 40 minute drive.

  • I-17, I-10, US-60, and Loop 202 all run through the city
  • Sky Harbor International Airport is located within Phoenix city limits
  • 10-20 minutes to most other central Valley cities
  • Close to Papago Park, South Mountain, and the Piestewa Peak trail system
  • Home to the largest concentration of downtown offices and employers in the Valley

FAQ

Common Questions About Phoenix

What is it like living in Phoenix, Arizona?

Phoenix is a large, diverse city made up of many distinct neighborhoods, ranging from historic and walkable to newer and suburban.

What types of homes are available in Phoenix?

Everything from historic bungalows and mid-century ranch homes to downtown condos and newer single-family construction.

Does Phoenix have new construction?

Some, mostly infill projects downtown and pockets of new development in North Phoenix, though less than in the outer suburbs.

How far is Phoenix from Downtown Phoenix?

Depends on the neighborhood, since Phoenix is the city downtown sits in, some areas are minutes away, others 30-40 minutes.

How far is Phoenix from Sky Harbor Airport?

Sky Harbor is located within Phoenix city limits, so most neighborhoods are within a 10-20 minute drive.

What are some popular neighborhoods in Phoenix?

Willo, Ahwatukee, and Downtown Phoenix are among the most searched, though the city has dozens of distinct areas.

What is there to do in Phoenix?

Roosevelt Row, South Mountain Park, the Heard Museum, and the Desert Botanical Garden are popular destinations.

What should I know before moving to Phoenix?

The city is large and varied, so it's worth spending time in a few different neighborhoods before deciding which part of Phoenix fits you best.
