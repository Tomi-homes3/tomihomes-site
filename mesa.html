About Mesa

What It's Actually Like Living in Mesa, AZ

Mesa is the third-largest city in Arizona, and it covers a lot of ground, literally and in terms of housing options. Established neighborhoods near Downtown Mesa date back decades, with larger lots and mature landscaping that's harder to find in newer Valley suburbs. Further east, the city stretches out toward Apache Junction and the Superstition Mountains, with newer subdivisions and more open space.

Downtown Mesa has seen real investment over the past several years, with a light rail extension, new restaurants, and redevelopment around the historic Main Street corridor. It's a different feel from the rest of the city, more walkable and increasingly popular with buyers who want an urban option without Phoenix or Tempe prices.

Moving to Mesa Arizona often appeals to buyers who want more square footage or lot size for their budget than they'd get in Tempe, Scottsdale, or Gilbert, without giving up freeway access or proximity to the rest of the Valley.

An Honest Look

Living in Mesa: What to Weigh

Worth Knowing

  • Generally larger lots and more affordable prices than nearby Tempe or Gilbert
  • Light rail access in and around Downtown Mesa
  • A genuinely revitalized, walkable downtown core
  • Close to hiking and outdoor recreation in the Superstition Mountains
  • Good freeway access via US-60 and Loop 202

Things to Consider

  • Some older neighborhoods have aging infrastructure
  • The city is large and spread out, so commute times vary a lot by area
  • Newer development on the east side means longer drives to central Phoenix
  • Mobile home and manufactured housing communities are more common here than in some neighboring cities
  • Some parts of the city have less tree cover and shade than more established Valley suburbs

New Construction

Building in Mesa

New construction in Mesa is concentrated in the eastern part of the city, in large master-planned communities like Eastmark. These areas tend to offer newer floor plans and more efficient construction than Mesa's established, closer-in neighborhoods.

If you're considering a new-build home in Mesa, you're welcome to have your own real estate representation, even when buying directly from a builder. It's worth thinking about who represents your interests before you visit a model home or register with a builder's sales office.
Schools & Education

School Districts Serving Mesa

Mesa is served primarily by one large public school district, along with several charter options.

  • Mesa Public Schools, the largest school district in Arizona
  • Gilbert Public Schools (parts of southeast Mesa)
  • A number of charter school options throughout the city

Mesa Community College and Arizona State University's Polytechnic campus both serve the area.

We're not able to advise on which schools or districts are the "best" fit, since that depends entirely on your family's needs. We're happy to point you toward district boundary maps and third-party research tools like GreatSchools.org so you can look into specific schools yourself.
Location

Getting Around Mesa

Mesa sits in the East Valley, bordered by Tempe and Scottsdale to the west, Gilbert to the south, and Apache Junction to the east.

  • US-60 (Superstition Freeway) runs through the city
  • Loop 202 serves the western and southern parts of Mesa
  • About 25-30 minutes to Downtown Phoenix
  • About 20-25 minutes to Sky Harbor Airport
  • Close to Saguaro Lake and the Superstition Mountains

FAQ

Common Questions About Mesa

What is it like living in Mesa, Arizona?

Mesa is a large, spread-out city offering more space and generally lower prices, with a revitalized downtown core.

What types of homes are available in Mesa?

A wide range, from established single-family homes to new construction in east Mesa and mobile home communities.

Does Mesa have new construction?

Yes, particularly in east Mesa communities like Eastmark.

How far is Mesa from Downtown Phoenix?

About 25 to 30 minutes under normal traffic conditions.

How far is Mesa from Phoenix Sky Harbor Airport?

About 20 to 25 minutes.

What are some popular neighborhoods in Mesa?

Downtown Mesa, Las Sendas, and Eastmark are among the more searched areas.

What is there to do in Mesa?

The Downtown Mesa Arts District, Usery Mountain Regional Park, and Sloan Park are popular destinations.

What should I know before moving to Mesa?

The city is large, so commute times and neighborhood character vary a lot depending on which part of Mesa you're considering.
