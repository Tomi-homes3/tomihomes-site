**Do sellers have to disclose a haunted house in Arizona?**
Arizona law doesn't mention ghosts at all. Under A.R.S. 32-2156, sellers and agents can't face legal action for not disclosing a death or felony that happened in a home.

It's October, the porch skeletons are out, and someone in your group chat just sent a link to a "haunted" house for sale. Which brings up one of the most fun Arizona real estate questions we get all year: if a house has a spooky past, does the seller have to tell you?

The answer surprises a lot of people. Arizona has a specific law about this, and it leans toward the seller. Here's what it says, what it doesn't, and what to do if you're buying or selling a home with a story.

## What Arizona law says about "stigmatized" homes

A home with a past that bothers people, but doesn't change anything physical about the house, is often called a stigmatized property. Arizona covers this in [A.R.S. 32-2156](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/32/02156.htm).

The law says a seller, landlord, or real estate agent can't be sued, prosecuted, or face a complaint against their license for not disclosing that a property:

- Is or was the site of a natural death, suicide, homicide, or any other felony
- Was owned or lived in by someone exposed to HIV, diagnosed with AIDS, or diagnosed with another disease that isn't spread by sharing a home
- Is located near a sex offender

It also says that not disclosing these things isn't a reason to cancel the sale.

## So what about ghosts?

The law never uses the words haunted, ghost, or paranormal. There's no Arizona disclosure line for "the hallway has a weird cold spot."

Since the law already protects sellers who don't mention a death in the home, most of the stories behind a "haunted" house fall under it anyway. A ghost story isn't a problem with the roof, the plumbing, or the foundation.

## What Arizona sellers still need to disclose

This is where people get tripped up. That law covers a short list of specific topics. It doesn't let sellers off the hook for everything.

Most Arizona sellers fill out a Seller's Property Disclosure Statement, or SPDS, a form from the [Arizona Association of REALTORS](https://www.aaronline.com/). It covers the physical condition of the home, like:

- Roof leaks and repairs
- Plumbing, electrical, and AC issues
- Water damage and leaks
- Pests, including termites and scorpions
- Pool and spa equipment

If the "ghost" is really a door that swings shut because the house settled, or footsteps in the attic that turn out to be a roof rat, that's a physical issue. Physical issues belong on the disclosure.

### What if a buyer asks directly?

The law protects a seller who stays quiet. It doesn't say anything about a seller who gives a dishonest answer to a direct question. If a buyer asks you point blank whether something happened in the house, talk to your agent or a real estate attorney before you answer.

## Buying a home? How to look into its history

If a home's past matters to you, you're allowed to ask. A few ways to do your homework:

- Have your agent ask the listing agent, in writing
- Search the address online, including old news stories
- Chat with the neighbors on your second showing
- Check Arizona's public sex offender registry if that's a concern for you

After that, it comes down to your own comfort level. Some buyers walk away from a home with a story. Others don't mind at all, especially when the house checks every other box.

## Selling a home with a story?

- Fill out the SPDS completely and honestly
- Tell your agent what you know, so nothing catches you off guard
- Keep your marketing focused on the home itself

## FAQ

### Do you have to disclose a death in a house in Arizona?
No. Under A.R.S. 32-2156, sellers and agents can't face legal action for not disclosing that a property was the site of a natural death, suicide, homicide, or felony.

### Can I back out of buying a house if I find out someone died there?
Not for that reason alone. The law says not disclosing a death isn't grounds to cancel the sale. Your purchase contract may give you other options during the inspection period, so talk to your agent.

### What is a stigmatized property?
It's a home with a past that makes some buyers uneasy, like a crime or a death, even though nothing is physically wrong with the house.
