About Central Phoenix

What It's Actually Like Living in Central Phoenix, AZ

Central Phoenix is where the city's history actually shows. Neighborhoods like Willo, Encanto-Palmcroft, and Coronado were built mostly between the 1920s and 1950s, and many retain their original bungalow and Tudor-style architecture, tree-lined streets, and small-lot character, a sharp contrast to the master-planned suburbs that dominate the rest of the Valley.

Downtown itself has changed substantially over the past decade. The Roosevelt Row Arts District has grown into a genuine gallery and restaurant scene, and the area around Chase Field and Footprint Center has added residential high-rises and apartments that didn't exist a generation ago. The light rail line running through Central Phoenix makes this one of the only parts of the Valley where getting around without a car is genuinely realistic.

Moving to Central Phoenix typically appeals to buyers who want walkability, historic character, and a short commute to downtown employers, generally in exchange for smaller lots and older homes that may need updating.

An Honest Look

Living in Central Phoenix: What to Weigh

Worth Knowing

  • The most walkable part of Phoenix, with genuine light rail access
  • Historic architecture and mature, tree-lined streets
  • Short commute to downtown employers and Sky Harbor Airport
  • A real and growing arts and dining scene
  • Strong, historically stable demand for well-located historic homes

Things to Consider

  • Smaller lots than newer suburban communities
  • Many homes are older and may need updating or historic-district approval for changes
  • Street parking can be limited in the densest pockets
  • Home prices in the most desirable historic districts run at a premium
  • Some surrounding blocks are less renovated than the core historic districts
Real Estate in Central Phoenix

What's Available

Central Phoenix's housing stock is dominated by historic single-family homes, with a growing share of downtown condos and high-rises.

Historic Bungalows

The defining housing type in districts like Willo and Coronado, mostly built 1920s-1950s.

Historic District Homes

Larger historic properties in Encanto-Palmcroft, often requiring historic preservation approval for exterior changes.

Downtown Condos & High-Rises

A growing segment near Roosevelt Row and the sports venues.

Infill New Construction

Occasional new single-family or townhome infill on vacant historic-district lots.

Townhomes

A smaller but growing option in and around downtown.

New Construction

Building in Central Phoenix

New construction in Central Phoenix is almost entirely infill, either downtown condo and apartment development, or the occasional new single-family home on a vacant lot within a historic district, since the area is largely built out.

If you're considering a new-build home in Central Phoenix, you're welcome to have your own real estate representation, even when buying directly from a builder. It's worth thinking about who represents your interests before you visit a model home or register with a builder's sales office.
Schools & Education

School Districts Serving Central Phoenix

Central Phoenix is served by several public school districts, since the area spans a number of smaller district boundaries.

  • Phoenix Union High School District
  • Osborn Elementary School District
  • Madison Elementary School District
  • A number of charter and private school options

Arizona State University's Downtown Phoenix campus and Grand Canyon University are both located in or near Central Phoenix.

We're not able to advise on which schools or districts are the "best" fit, since that depends entirely on your family's needs. We're happy to point you toward district boundary maps and third-party research tools like GreatSchools.org so you can look into specific schools yourself.
Location

Getting Around Central Phoenix

Central Phoenix sits in the heart of the city, bordered by Arcadia to the northeast and South Phoenix across the Salt River.

  • I-10 and I-17 both serve the area
  • Light rail runs directly through Central Phoenix
  • About 10-15 minutes to Sky Harbor Airport
  • Close to the Melrose District and Encanto Park
  • A short drive to Arcadia and Tempe

FAQ

Common Questions About Central Phoenix

What is it like living in Central Phoenix, Arizona?

Central Phoenix is the city's most walkable, historic area, with light rail access and a growing downtown arts scene.

What types of homes are available in Central Phoenix?

Mostly historic bungalows and craftsman homes, plus a growing number of downtown condos and high-rises.

Does Central Phoenix have new construction?

Mostly infill, downtown condo development or occasional new single-family homes on vacant historic-district lots.

How far is Central Phoenix from Downtown Phoenix?

You're already there, most of Central Phoenix is within a few minutes of downtown.

How far is Central Phoenix from Phoenix Sky Harbor Airport?

About 10 to 15 minutes.

What are some popular neighborhoods in Central Phoenix?

Willo, Encanto-Palmcroft, and Coronado are among the most searched historic districts.

What is there to do in Central Phoenix?

Roosevelt Row, Encanto Park, the Heard Museum, and downtown sports and event venues.

What should I know before moving to Central Phoenix?

Many homes are older and some fall within historic districts with design guidelines, worth researching before you buy if you're planning changes.
